QuickOne Scanner — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026 · iOS and Android applications

QuickOne Scanner is a document scanner for iPhone, iPad and Android. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with your information, on both platforms. It is written to be accurate rather than reassuring; where a third party is involved, it says so, and where the two platforms genuinely differ, it says that too.

The short version. Your scanned documents never leave your device. QuickOne Scanner has no account system, no cloud sync, no ads, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. We do not operate a server, and we never receive your documents, your images, or the text recognised from them.

1. Information we collect

None. The developer of QuickOne Scanner collects no personal information. There is no sign-up, no login, no account, and no user profile. We do not use any analytics, advertising, attribution, or crash-reporting SDK. We have no server that receives data from the app.

2. Your documents and scanned images

Scans, page images, recognised text, tags, folder names, and document titles are stored only in the app's private storage area on your device. Other apps cannot read them. They are never uploaded anywhere.

All processing happens locally on your device: edge detection, image enhancement, the Original / Grayscale / Black & white filters, cropping, text recognition, the full-text search index, and the keyword rules that suggest a tag for a document. None of it involves a network request, and none of it is sent anywhere for processing.

When you use Share, Export, or the ZIP backup, you choose where a copy goes. Once you hand a file to another app — email, cloud storage, messaging — that app's own privacy policy applies to the copy you gave it.

3. Network access

On iPhone and iPad

The app makes no network requests at all. There is nothing to download and nothing to send. The capture screen is Apple's own VisionKit document camera, which ships with iOS, and both text-recognition models are compiled into the app — so the very first scan works on a device that has never been online.

On Android

QuickOne Scanner declares the INTERNET permission. It is not used to send your documents anywhere. It is there for one thing:

  • The capture screen. The camera view with automatic page detection is a component of Google Play services, and Google Play services downloads it the first time you scan. After that first download, scanning works offline.

Text recognition needs no download on either platform. Both recognition models the app uses — Latin and Chinese — are compiled into the app itself, so OCR runs offline from the very first scan.

4. Third-party components

On iPhone and iPad, the only system component the app uses for capture is Apple's own, and it processes on your device. On Android, QuickOne Scanner uses components provided by Google; we do not control what those components report to Google, and Google's use of any such data is governed by Google's own privacy policy, not this one.

On neither platform is there an analytics, advertising, attribution or crash-reporting SDK.

For transparency, and this applies to the Android build only: Google's ML Kit and Play services libraries include Google's own diagnostic and usage-reporting components, and it is those libraries that add the ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE permission to the app — we did not request it. Any such reporting is Google's, is described in Google's Privacy Policy, and reaches the developer of QuickOne Scanner not at all. The contents of your documents are not part of it. The iOS build contains no Google library and nothing equivalent.

5. Permissions

It requests no photo-library permission — importing a photo goes through the system picker, which hands over only the file you chose — and no location, contacts, microphone or notification permission. There is no App Tracking Transparency prompt, because the app does not track you across other companies' apps and websites and has no advertising identifier to ask for.

It does not request storage or photo-library permissions either; scans are kept in app-private storage, and importing a photo goes through Android's system picker, which grants access to the single file you chose and nothing else.

6. Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device for as long as you keep it. Deleting a document in the app removes its page images and its recognised text from your device. Uninstalling QuickOne Scanner removes everything the app stored.

There is no cloud backup, and that cuts both ways. Because nothing is uploaded, a lost, reset, or broken phone means the scans on it are gone — we cannot restore them, because we never had them. Use Settings → Export library as ZIP to keep your own copy somewhere safe.

7. Payments

There are none, on either platform. QuickOne Scanner is free, with no subscription, no in-app purchase, no scan allowance, no ads and no watermark, so the app never handles payment information and no payment processor is involved.

8. Children

QuickOne Scanner is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. Since the app collects nothing from anyone, there is nothing to delete on request — but see section 6 for how to remove data from the device itself.
