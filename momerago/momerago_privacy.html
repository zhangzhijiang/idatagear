Part 1 — Privacy Policy

Who we are

Momera GO records a hike as you walk it — the route, photos and videos pinned where you took them, voice memos and notes — and turns it into a journey you can replay. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Android, and is built to work where there is no signal.

Momera GO is published by iDataGear Inc, a company incorporated in British Columbia, Canada. Where this policy says we or us, it means iDataGear Inc.

There is no account to create and no sign-up. We do not operate a server that receives your content, and we hold no database in which you have a record.

What the app keeps on your device

The app writes the following to private storage on your device — an area other apps cannot read. It stays there until you delete it or uninstall the app.

What Why Where it is kept

None of this is uploaded to us. We cannot read it, cannot recover it for you, and are not able to hand it to anyone else, because it never reaches us.

You can import a Google Maps Timeline export to rebuild past trips. You supply the file yourself; the app reads it on your device, and neither the file nor anything derived from it is uploaded. Delete the imported trips in the app and they are gone.

Building a journey from photos uses the system photo picker. The picker hands the app only the items you select — the app is not granted access to your library, and on iOS no photo-library permission is requested at all. Location read from a photo's own metadata is used to place it on the map and stays with the journey on your device.

Step counting reads the device's own step sensor through the activity-recognition permission. The count is stored with the hike on your device. No health or fitness platform is read or written — not Apple Health, not Google Fit.

The app can open a .gpx file another app hands it and can export your route as .gpx or GeoJSON. Import reads the file on your device; export writes a file you then decide what to do with.

Analytics and crash reporting

The app uses Google Firebase for two things: Crashlytics, which reports crashes, and Analytics, which counts how features are used. Both are processed by Google on our behalf.

A crash report contains the state of the program when it failed — the error, the call stack, the device model, the operating-system version and the app version. It is generated automatically by the failure and tells us which line of our code to fix.

The analytics events are diagnostic counters for the reliability of location recording — that a track started, stopped or was recovered, or that the position stream stalled and reopened. No coordinates, no route and no place name is included in any of them.

This applies to the Android build only. The iOS build ships without Firebase configuration, so it initialises no Firebase service and sends no crash report and no analytics event at all.
