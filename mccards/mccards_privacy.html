McCards — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

McCards is an offline wallet for loyalty cards, coupons, shopping lists and receipts. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with your information. It is written to be accurate rather than reassuring; where a third party is involved, it says so.

The short version. What you put into McCards stays on your phone. Your card numbers, PINs, coupons and photos are never sent anywhere, are never shared with advertising networks or data brokers, and are not visible to us. The one exception is the AI features, and only when you use them: the shopping-list description you ask about, or the text recognised on a receipt you ask to clean up, is sent through our server to Google's Gemini AI for an answer. To count AI use and to know whether you subscribe to McCards Premium, the app has an account on our server — an anonymous one, unless you choose to sign in with Google, which is optional.

1. Information we collect

An account ID, an AI usage record and, if you subscribe, your subscription's status. Nothing has to be signed up for, and McCards never asks for your phone number.

  • When the phone is online, the app signs in to Firebase Authentication, a Google service, anonymously. That creates a random identifier for this installation of the app. It is not linked to your name, email address, phone number or Google account.
  • Signing in with Google is optional. If you choose to, in Settings or when the app offers it, the anonymous ID is linked to your Google account, and Firebase Authentication keeps your Google account's email address, name and account identifier. That is what lets your AI actions follow you to another phone. You can sign out, or delete the account, at any time (section 9).
  • Our server keeps a record under your ID for the AI features: how many AI actions you have left, a counter used to limit how many requests an account can make in an hour, and when the record was created and last changed.
  • If you subscribe to McCards Premium, our server keeps what Google Play tells it about the subscription: the purchase token, which plan, its status, when the current period ends, whether it renews, and how many AI actions have been used this month. Google Play handles the payment itself (section 11).
  • When an AI taster is granted to a Google account, our server keeps a one-way fingerprint (a hash) of that Google account's identifier and the date, so the taster is given only once per account — even if the account is deleted and created again. The fingerprint cannot be turned back into your account or email address.
  • Our server's logs record your ID when an AI request or a purchase check fails, so the failure can be investigated. They never record the text that was sent, or your email address.

The text you send to an AI feature passes through our server on its way to Google and back. Our server does not store it or log it.

We run no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising SDK.

We do not collect your location. When you choose a flyer region, the app can use your phone's approximate location, or the first three characters of your postal code, to offer the regions near you. That is worked out on your phone and is not sent to us or to anyone else (see section 8).

2. Card numbers, PINs and barcodes

This is the section people most want to read, so it is near the top.

When you add a loyalty or membership card, McCards stores the card's barcode content exactly as scanned, along with the barcode type, any PIN you choose to enter, and any photo of the card you import or take. All of it is written to the app's own private storage on the device.

  • Card numbers and PINs are never transmitted. They are not sent to us, to the store, or to anyone else.
  • They are stored in the app's private area, which other apps on the phone cannot read. They are not separately encrypted beyond the protection Android gives app-private storage and whatever device-level encryption your phone uses. If someone has an unlocked phone, they can open McCards.
  • McCards has no app lock or biometric prompt in this release. Your phone's own screen lock is the protection.

3. Photos and images

Card photos, coupon images and receipt scans are stored in the app's private storage. They are never uploaded, including when you use an AI feature.

When you import a picture, McCards uses the Android system photo picker, which gives the app access only to the specific images you choose. The app requests no storage or media permission and cannot browse your gallery.

4. Camera

McCards does not request the camera permission.

Scanning a barcode and scanning a receipt are both handled by components that run inside Google Play Services, which operates the camera under its own identity and returns only the result to McCards. The app never receives a camera stream and never stores one. Reading a barcode out of a photo you already have is done on the device with Google's ML Kit, which needs no permission at all.

5. Text recognition

McCards reads text from coupon and receipt images to suggest an expiry date, a total, a purchase date and a store. This runs entirely on the device using Google's ML Kit, with the recognition models bundled in the app. The images are never sent anywhere, and the recognised text is sent only if you ask for it to be cleaned up, below. The recognised text is stored on the device so you can search your receipts.

Clean up with AI. On a receipt, you can ask for a tidier, line-by-line version of the recognised text. Only when you tap Clean up with AI, the text recognised on that one receipt, and where each piece of it sits on the page, is sent through our server to Google's Gemini API. The photo is not sent. The answer is saved on your phone next to the original text, which is kept unchanged.

6. Why the app requests internet access

McCards declares the INTERNET permission for the purposes below. Only the AI features send anything you have created, and only when you use them:

  • AI features. When you use Describe it on a shopping list, the description you typed or spoke is sent. When you use Clean up with AI on a receipt, that receipt's recognised text is sent. Either goes to our server (a Google Cloud Function) with the anonymous ID from section 1, and from there to Google's Gemini API. The answer comes back to your phone. Nothing is sent until you tap the button.
  • Your account and AI balance. When the phone is online, the app signs in to Firebase (anonymously, or with the Google account you chose) and reads its AI balance and subscription status, so it can show what is left.
  • Subscriptions. When you subscribe, and each time the app opens, it asks Google Play which McCards subscriptions your Play account holds and sends their purchase tokens to our server, which checks them with Google Play. No payment details are involved.
  • Google Play Services components. The barcode scanner and document scanner are downloaded by Play Services on first use.
  • The store catalogue. McCards ships with a list of common Canadian retail chains used to suggest stores and recognise them on receipts. The app can check for and download a newer version of this list. It is a plain file download: your data is not part of the request, and the file contains no personal information.
  • Flyers. When you choose a region in the Deals tab, the app downloads that region's flyer file, and a small settings file for flyers, from a public GitHub repository. Once you have a region, it checks for newer ones at most once a day when you use the Deals or Shopping tab, or whenever you ask it to. Newer flyers download by themselves only on Wi-Fi; on mobile data the app asks first. Nothing is downloaded before you open the Deals tab. The requests contain no personal information.
  • Flyer pictures. When you tap an item in a flyer, the app loads that item's picture from the flyer publisher's image server (f.wishabi.net, operated by Flipp). Pictures are loaded only when you open them — by tapping an item, or by swiping to the next one from a picture — one at a time, and are not saved on your phone.

Each of these is an ordinary web request. The server that answers it — Google, GitHub or Flipp — sees your phone's IP address, as any website does. Apart from the AI features, nothing you have stored in McCards is part of the request.

Everything you store in McCards works offline. If the phone never goes online, only the AI features, catalogue updates, flyer downloads and flyer pictures stop working.

7. Third-party components

McCards uses Google Play Services and Google ML Kit for on-device scanning and text recognition. Google's handling of the requests your device makes to its own services is governed by Google's privacy policy, not ours. We receive nothing from those interactions.

The AI features use Google's Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore and Cloud Functions) and Google's Gemini API. Text you send to an AI feature is processed by Google under the Gemini API terms. Optional sign-in uses Google Sign-In, and subscriptions use Google Play Billing; Google's privacy policy governs both on Google's side.

The app contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK.

8. Permissions

The app declares two permissions:

  • INTERNET, for the reasons in section 6.
  • ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, your phone's approximate location. It is asked for only when you choose a flyer region and tap Use my location, so that the app offers just the regions near you. It is read once, used on your phone to measure the distance to each region's towns, and then discarded: it is not stored, and it is not sent to us or to anyone else. You can refuse it and type the first three characters of your postal code instead, which is used the same way, on your phone only.

Android's own libraries add ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, which reports only whether a connection exists and whether it is metered, so flyers download by themselves only on Wi-Fi. Google Play Billing adds com.android.vending.BILLING, which lets the app show Google Play's purchase screen. Google's sign-in and security libraries add USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINT and READ_GSERVICES; McCards itself never uses biometrics or asks for a fingerprint. None of these is a permission the app asks you for.

McCards does not request the camera, storage, media, precise location, background location, contacts or notification permissions.

9. Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on the device until you remove it. Deleting a card, coupon, list or receipt deletes its photos too.

One thing is deleted automatically: expired coupons, and their images, a configurable number of days after they expire. How long they are kept is shown in Settings, where you can change it. Coupons you have marked as used are never deleted automatically.

Uninstalling the app deletes everything on the phone. Because none of your cards, coupons, lists or receipts is stored off the device, we cannot restore them — we never had them. Use Settings → Back up everything to save a copy somewhere you control before switching phones.

Deleting your account. If you signed in with Google, use Settings → Account → Delete account. It deletes your account, your email address and name, and your AI usage record from our server straight away, and signs you out. Two things are kept, unlinked from you: the record of a Google Play subscription you bought (it is a record of a sale, and it is how the subscription can be restored to a new account), and the one-way fingerprint described in section 1. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription — cancel it in Google Play.

Without signing in, or without the app, you can ask us to delete your data at idatagear.com/mccards-delete.html. Uninstalling does not remove the anonymous record on our server, which holds nothing but an AI balance, a request counter and two dates; a new installation gets a new anonymous ID and starts a new record.

10. Backups

The backup file McCards creates contains all of your data, including card numbers, PINs and photos, and it is not encrypted. Once you save or send it, it is as private as wherever you put it. Treat it the way you would treat a photograph of your wallet.

11. Payments

McCards Premium is a subscription sold through Google Play. The app never asks for, handles, or stores payment information: no card number, billing address or payment credential is ever entered into McCards or transmitted by it. Google Play takes the payment under Google's own privacy policy, and tells our server only what section 1 lists — never your payment details. You manage or cancel the subscription in Google Play.

12. Children

McCards is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from children. The only information it keeps off the phone is what section 1 lists.
